The Taste of Wild Strawberries

Some encounters never become a life together. They become part of us.

Christophe no longer expected an encounter that could turn his life upside down. Then came Juliana. A few days in Paris, two lives separated by an ocean, and a story whose ending becomes the beginning of another journey.

The first glance

Read a passage here, without signing up.

Then Christophe looked up.

He saw her.

Her light brown hair, touched with lighter strands, fell freely over her shoulders. Soft features, dark and expressive eyes. She was beautiful.

But it was not really her beauty that held his gaze.

She was not trying to attract attention. Yet Christophe had immediately been drawn to her presence, to that utterly natural way she had of being there, without ever trying to impose herself.

And then there was her smile. A broad, spontaneous smile that seemed to spread across her whole face, all the way to her eyes.

She talked with her friend, sometimes accompanying her words with a gesture. Then she listened. Laughed. Became attentive again.

She seemed unaware that anyone might be watching her. And yet Christophe could not help looking at her.

He looked away, almost embarrassed by this sudden attraction, and tried to pick up the thread of the conversation. Above all, he did not want her to notice his persistent gaze, still less to feel bothered by it.

But a few seconds later, his eyes returned to her. Despite himself. He simply wanted to look at her again, to find that smile, that presence that had immediately touched him.

Hassan was still talking about the breakup he could not understand. Christophe was listening. At least he was trying to.

His gaze kept returning to her.

There was something deeply alive about this woman. A way of being wholly there, in a conversation, in a laugh, in the moment.

He knew nothing about her. Not her name. Not her age. Not the city she lived in. Not even the language she spoke.

He knew nothing of the life behind that smile.

For now, all he saw was a woman sitting a few tables away. And a smile that, for a reason he did not yet understand, kept him from looking entirely elsewhere.

One thing finally became clear to him.

He did not want to leave this restaurant without having spoken to her.

He leaned towards the table, moving closer to Juliana until he was facing her. Without a plan. Without really knowing what he was going to say.

She looked up at him.

For the first time, their eyes truly met.

Up close, Christophe found again the smile that had so drawn him in. He also discovered her eyes, bright, curious, almost amused to see this stranger come over to speak to her.

He hesitated for a second.

– Excuse me… May I take your photograph?

Juliana looked surprised. There was clearly something unexpected about the request.

Then she smiled again.

– Of course.

Christophe took the photograph. He could have thanked her and left it at that. But he had no desire to.

He took the opportunity to keep the conversation going. A few words led to others.

He learned that she was Brazilian, that she came from Brasília and that she was in Paris for a training course with the firefighters. She told him about her work, her stay, this city she was discovering with such enthusiasm.

She was leaving in three days.

Three days.

Christophe looked at her friend, then back at Juliana.

– And where is your husband?

Before Juliana even had time to answer, her friend burst out laughing.

– But she’s single!

Juliana rolled her eyes with a smile, caught between amusement and embarrassment. Christophe laughed too.

– Well… so am I.

This time, they looked at each other for a few seconds longer.

Nothing spectacular. Just a smile exchanged between two strangers in a Parisian restaurant.

Christophe asked for her number so he could send her the photograph. She gave it to him.

Everything had been astonishingly simple. No strategy. No calculated seduction.
