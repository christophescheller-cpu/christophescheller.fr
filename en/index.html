THE NOVEL

Can a story leave a profound mark on us when it barely had time to exist?

Christophe no longer expected to meet someone who could turn his life upside down.

Then came Juliana.

A few days in Paris. An unexpected sense of connection. And soon, the distance between Paris and Brasília, the silences, the waiting, and the strange impossibility of fully living a story that nevertheless refuses to fade.

Because some encounters never become a life together. They become part of us.

Le Goût des Fraises Sauvages tells the story of what remains when a relationship ends but what it awakened lives on.

Through memory, longing, desire, letting go and rebuilding, Christophe gradually tries to understand why some people pass through our lives for only a few moments yet leave a lasting mark.

An intimate novel about love, absence and those encounters that sometimes force us to see who we were — and who we might still become — in a different light.

Inspired by a true story, Le Goût des Fraises Sauvages explores the fragile boundary between what we actually lived, what we hoped to live, and what remains despite everything.

THROUGH THE REWRITING

The latest revisions to the novel

Since the originally published version, I have extensively reworked Le Goût des Fraises Sauvages. I wanted to bring the story more fully to life and explore Christophe’s experience in greater depth, while leaving more room for contradictions and unanswered questions.

I began by developing the characters and scenes. We learn more about Christophe’s life before the encounter, his habits and his sense of balance. Juliana and Karima also become more vivid through their gestures, expressions and ways of being. My aim was to help readers get to know them better and feel what draws Christophe to each of them.

Above all, I expanded the development of his relationship with Juliana: their everyday exchanges, the place they make for each other in their lives, their families, learning French, travel preparations and plans for the future. I wanted to show more clearly why Christophe had believed in this story, and how both of them had made it real despite the distance.

This led me to explore the breakup and its consequences more deeply. The pain is more tangible: the difficult nights, the tears, the cancelled trip, the future he no longer knows how to replace. I also allowed his anger, disappointment and difficulty understanding the silence to emerge. I wanted his journey towards peace to make real room for the wound.

The story with Karima has also been reworked. Christophe’s indecision is more apparent: unable to choose between two possible lives, he eventually lets her leave without trying to hold her back. This passage gives greater weight to his own responsibility and sheds a different light on how he sees Juliana: he too knows how difficult it is to give up one life in order to choose another.

Finally, I revised the ending to make Christophe more cautious in his interpretations. He considers several reasons for Juliana’s departure, but accepts that he may never know what really happened within her. His peace no longer depends so much on an explanation he needs to believe.

I also wanted to show that moving forward could mean rediscovering the pleasure of living, of looking at someone, of loving again one day, while preserving the trace of that encounter. And if Juliana were to return, feelings would no longer be enough: trust, too, would need to be rebuilt.

Through these changes, I have tried to preserve all the beauty of the story while fully acknowledging what it cost Christophe. The new version expresses this conviction more strongly: a story can have been deeply sincere without managing to become a shared life. And we can retain its value without having to soften its ending.

ABOUT THE AUTHOR

Christophe Scheller

Le Goût des Fraises Sauvages is Christophe’s first novel.

It grew out of an encounter that profoundly marked his life.

What was initially meant to be an account of a personal story became, through writing, a reflection on love, time, and the room our lives can — or cannot — make for our feelings.

How much of this story comes from lived experience? How much belongs to fiction?

Perhaps only those who recognise themselves in these pages know the answer.

The author has chosen not to answer that question.

It is for the reader to imagine where one ends and the other begins.

By the time he finished writing this first novel, Christophe had reached this conviction:

Facts belong to biography.
Truth belongs to the novel.

FROM THE WORLD OF PROJECTS TO WRITING

A book I had never thought of writing…
but that it had become impossible not to write.

For thirty years, I worked in a world where we can usually understand problems, analyse constraints and look for solutions.

Then I encountered something that could not be solved.

How can someone continue to transform your life when they are no longer part of it?

I had never imagined writing a book. I am an engineer. I spent most of my professional life analysing, managing projects, making decisions and seeking solutions.

And then there was an encounter. One that, long after it ended, kept asking me a question no method could answer.

So I began to write.

Not to publish.
Not even to tell a story.
To understand.

Little by little, memories became pages. Pages became a story. And the story became a novel.

A novel about love, memory, absence and those encounters that do not necessarily become a shared life… but can become part of us.

I do not know what you will find in this book. Perhaps a love story. Perhaps a reflection on memory and what we do with our memories. Or perhaps simply something that reminds you of someone.

Because we have probably all, at some point, met someone who changed something within us.

Today, I am happy finally to share this book with those who have known me for a long time — sometimes for several decades — but who may discover here a side of me they know less well.

READERS’ WORDS

When the book begins to belong to its readers

Writing is a solitary experience. Then comes the moment when a book leaves its author and meets its readers.

Some messages I received after readers finished Goût des Fraises Sauvages particularly moved me. They show that behind Christophe and Juliana’s story, each of us may sometimes find something of our own.

Thank you to everyone who takes the time to tell me what this story has awakened in them.
